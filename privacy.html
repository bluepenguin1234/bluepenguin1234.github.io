Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 2026

Applies to Nivli for iPhone, version 3.0 and later. Earlier versions of Nivli were a different product; the previous policy no longer applies.

The short version: Nivli does not collect any data. The apps you choose, your workouts, your streak and your settings stay on your iPhone. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code. Once at launch the app downloads a small settings file from our website that holds its own wording; it carries nothing about you, in either direction. Nivli never learns the names of the apps you locked, and anything it reads from Apple Health stays on the device. The App Store privacy label for Nivli is Data Not Collected.

What Nivli stores, and where

Everything Nivli keeps is written into a storage area that belongs to the app on your iPhone (an App Group, so the app and its two Screen Time extensions can read the same file). None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. We have no way to see it.

  • The apps that wait. When you pick apps, categories or websites, iOS hands Nivli a set of opaque tokens. A token is a meaningless identifier that only iOS can resolve. Nivli stores the tokens so it can ask iOS to shield the same things tomorrow. It cannot read an app's name, icon, bundle ID or your usage from a token, and neither can we. When the app shows you the list, iOS draws the names and icons; Nivli only supplies the tokens.
  • Your workout log. For each workout: the date, the start time, the length in minutes, the kind (run, walk, strength and so on) and whether you logged it by hand or it came from Apple Health.
  • Your daily step totals. If you choose a step goal, one number per day: the total steps Apple Health reported for that day. Not individual steps or samples, just the day's total.
  • Your streak. Worked out on the device from the workout log, your step totals and your rest days. The current and longest streak are kept so the app can show them.
  • When each day was won. For a day your apps opened, Nivli writes down the moment it happened — one entry per day, the first one only. It is stored so the app can work out how many hours your apps stayed shut across a week. No screen, widget or notification in Nivli shows it, and Nivli does not score or compare it: it does not matter what time of day you earn your apps.
  • Your settings. Which rule wins your day (a workout, a step goal, or either), your minimum workout length and your step goal, your weekly rest days and any day you took off, whether Apple Health, reminders and the Sunday report are on, the reminder time, your appearance choice, whether strict mode is on and any change it is holding for 24 hours, the day you started your first week, the answers you gave during onboarding — your first name if you typed one, what brought you to Nivli, how often you move and your own guess at your phone hours — and whether the subscription is currently valid.

Nivli's data is stored in a file that is excluded from iCloud Backup and computer backups, because it can contain workouts read from Apple Health. Setting up a new iPhone means setting Nivli up again; your streak does not move between phones.

Screen Time (Family Controls)

Locking apps uses Apple's Screen Time API. Here is exactly what that means.

  • The authorization. On first run Nivli asks iOS for Screen Time authorization for this iPhone's own user (Apple calls this individual authorization). You approve it in a system prompt. Nivli has no parental controls and no family or child features, and it never requests authorization for another person's device.
  • What Nivli can do. Two things: show you Apple's own app picker, and ask iOS to put a shield over the apps, categories and sites you picked in that picker. A background extension re-applies the shield at midnight so a new day starts locked.
  • What Nivli cannot see. It cannot see which apps you chose, how long you use any app, what you do inside an app, your notifications, your browsing, or any Screen Time report. The selection arrives as opaque tokens and stays that way.
  • What is reported. Nothing. Nivli sends no information about your selection or your app use anywhere, because it has nowhere to send it.
  • Removing the shields. Clear your selection in Nivli's Settings, turn Screen Time authorization off, or delete the app, and iOS lifts the shields. Nivli also lifts them by itself when your subscription is not valid, when onboarding was never finished, when the selection is empty, on a rest day, and once you have worked out. It never locks anything you did not pick.

One honest limit: a shield stops an app from opening. It cannot close an app you already have open. iOS shows the Nivli lock screen the next time you open it.

Apple Health

Connecting Apple Health is optional. Nivli works perfectly well with workouts you type in yourself, or, if you choose a step-goal rule, without Health at all — though then nothing can be measured, so nothing is locked.

  • Read-only, workouts and step count. Nivli asks for permission to read workouts and your step count. It asks for nothing else, and it never writes anything to Health.
  • What it uses for workouts. For a workout on the current day: its start time, its length, its type, and its identifier so the same workout is not counted twice.
  • What it uses for steps. Today's step total, and the totals for past days already logged, so the app can work out your streak. Never individual steps or samples, just a total for each day.
  • Where it goes. Nowhere. Everything is read on your iPhone, turned into an entry in Nivli's own on-device log, and used to decide whether today is unlocked. It is never transmitted, and we never receive it. Apple's rules for HealthKit data say it must not be used for advertising, marketing or sale to anyone; Nivli does none of those, has no advertising, and has no one to sell to.
  • Background delivery. With your permission, Health can wake Nivli when a new workout is recorded or roughly once an hour when your step count changes — that is the shortest interval Apple allows for steps — so your apps can unlock without you opening the app. Nivli also re-reads your steps every time you open it, so opening the app is the surest way to see a just-reached goal. The wake-up carries the same data and is handled entirely on the device.
  • Turning it off. Open the Health app, then Sharing › Apps › Nivli, and switch Workouts and Steps off. You can also use iPhone Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Nivli, or turn Apple Health off inside Nivli's Settings. Nivli then stops importing. Data it already imported stays in Nivli's log until you reset it or delete the app.

Notifications

Notifications are optional and are asked for once, during onboarding. There are four kinds, every one of them scheduled locally on your iPhone:

  • a note that your apps have opened, when a workout or your step goal arrives from Apple Health while Nivli is closed;
  • an evening reminder at the time you choose, only on a day you have not won yet;
  • the Sunday-evening Your Tempo summary, if you leave it switched on, carrying that week’s own minutes and steps;
  • if you start a free trial, one reminder two days before it ends, saying what will be charged and where to cancel. It is removed the moment the subscription stops.

They contain nothing else. Nivli has no push server and receives no remote notifications; nothing about any of them is sent anywhere, because each one is scheduled by the app on your own device. Decline, or turn them off later in iPhone Settings › Notifications › Nivli, and the rest of the app works the same.

The widget and Your Tempo

The Home Screen and Lock Screen widget, and the weekly Your Tempo report, both use data Nivli already has on your iPhone — the same workout log, step totals and settings described above. Neither one measures anything new, and neither sends anything anywhere. The widget reads that data on the device to draw its ring and streak. Your Tempo is built on the device too, from the same log; tapping Share turns it into an image on your iPhone and hands it to iOS's own share sheet, which is yours to use or not.

Your Tempo is a report on your week's rhythm: of the days you planned to move, how many you won, along with your minutes, your steps as a distance and any record the week set. It says nothing about what time of day anything happened.

A small settings file

Once at launch, and at most once every six hours after that, Nivli downloads one file from our website: nivli-config.json, which holds the app's own wording and a few switches. It lets us fix a sentence or turn a screen off without shipping a new version.

  • It is a download, not an upload. The request is for a single static file that is identical for every copy of Nivli. It carries no account, no identifier, no setting of yours, no workout and nothing you typed into the app. There is no query string, no cookie and no analytics code attached to it.
  • What our host can see. The file is served by GitHub Pages. Like any web request, it reaches GitHub with your device's IP address, which GitHub may log under its own privacy statement. We do not run the server, we receive no log from it, and we have no way to connect a request to a person. It is never used to identify you, and it is never combined with anything else.
  • If it fails, nothing happens. No signal, no answer, a broken file: Nivli keeps the words it already has and carries on.

This is the only request Nivli makes that is not to Apple.

Purchases

Nivli is sold as an auto-renewable subscription through Apple’s in-app purchase system — monthly or yearly, both starting with seven days free. Apple handles the payment, your Apple Account, receipts, renewals and refunds, under Apple’s own privacy policy. Nivli learns two things from Apple: whether the subscription is currently valid on this device, and whether this Apple Account is still eligible for the free week (so it is never offered to someone who has already had one). It never sees your payment card, billing address or Apple Account details, and it keeps no purchase records anywhere except that valid-until date on your phone. Manage or cancel in iPhone Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.

Children

Nivli is a general-purpose app for adults managing their own screen time on their own iPhone. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone of any age, because we do not collect personal information at all. Nivli is not a parental-control app: it has no family or child features, it cannot be installed on or used to restrict someone else's device, and its Screen Time authorization covers only the iPhone it is running on.

Your choices

  • Unlock everything. Clear your selection under Settings › Apps that wait and nothing is shielded any more.
  • Take a day off. Set weekly rest days, or use Take today off, under Settings.
  • Stop Apple Health. Turn it off in Nivli's Settings, or in Health › Sharing › Apps › Nivli.
  • Stop notifications. Turn reminders off in Nivli's Settings, or in iPhone Settings › Notifications › Nivli.
  • Delete your data. Settings › Reset › Turn off Nivli and delete my data erases your workouts, streak, selection and settings from this iPhone.
  • Delete the app. Removing Nivli removes its storage with it, and iOS lifts the shields.

Since we never receive your data, there is no data we could hand over, correct or erase on request. The only copy is on your iPhone.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Nivli ever changes how data is handled, for example by adding a service run by us, this page and the App Store privacy label will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
