Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 12, 2026 · Applies to Nivli for iPhone, version 1.0 and later

Updated for version 1.1: details on things, and asking a question in your own words on iPhones with Apple Intelligence. What Nivli collects has not changed.

The short version: Nivli does not collect any data. Everything you enter stays in the app on your iPhone — and, if you choose to share some things with your household, in your own iCloud account, where only the people you invite can see it. There is no account with us, no server, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party code. The App Store privacy label for Nivli is Data Not Collected.

What Nivli stores, and where

Nivli keeps four kinds of information, all of which you create yourself:

  • Things — the items you track, such as “Change air filter”, with any notes you add.
  • Logs — the dates on which a thing happened, with an optional note and an optional photo.
  • Reminders — when a thing should happen next and how often it repeats.
  • Details — facts you save about a thing: size, brand, model, where it is kept, an expiry date, a link, who did the work, a note. A correction keeps the old value and where each value came from, so nothing is lost.

This information is stored in a database inside the app’s own storage area on your iPhone. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. We have no way to see it.

A small summary (thing titles and dates) is also written to a shared area on your phone so the Nivli widgets can display what is coming up. That area belongs to the app and stays on the device.

The one exception is the optional Household feature, described below. Things you have not switched to “Share with household” never leave your iPhone.

Details sync only for the things you share with a household: they go into the creator’s own iCloud with the rest of the shared data. The details of your personal things stay on your iPhone. Details are shown only on an unlocked iPhone, and Nivli does not put them into Spotlight.

Photos

You can attach one photo to a log entry — the label on a filter box, a receipt, a meter reading. Here is what happens to it:

  • Choosing a photo uses Apple’s photo picker, which runs outside the app. Nivli receives only the one photo you pick and never asks for access to your photo library.
  • Taking a photo uses the camera. iOS asks for camera permission the first time you tap Take photo, and Nivli uses the camera only at that moment. You can decline and still choose photos from your library.
  • Storage. The photo is reduced to at most 1600 pixels and saved inside Nivli’s database on your iPhone, alongside the entry. It is not sent to us, and Nivli never writes to your photo library: a photo chosen from the library stays there as before, and a photo taken with Nivli’s camera button exists only inside Nivli.
  • Text in photos. Nivli reads any text in the photo on your iPhone using Apple’s Vision framework — for example the size printed on a filter — and stores that text with the entry so you can search for it. This happens entirely on the device; no image or text is sent to Apple or to us for this.
  • Household. If the entry belongs to a thing you share with your household, the photo and its text sync to the household with the rest of the entry, into the creator’s iCloud, and the members can see it.
  • Removing. Edit the entry and remove the photo, or delete the entry; the photo is deleted with it.

Household (optional sharing through your own iCloud)

A household lets a family keep some things together — the air filter, the pets, the plants. It is optional, and nothing is shared until you switch a thing to Share with household.

  • Where the data is. Shared things, their details, their history (including any photos and the text read from them), and the display names members choose are stored in the iCloud account of the person who created the household (Apple’s CloudKit service, in that person’s private database). Each member’s iPhone keeps a copy so the app works offline. Nothing is stored with us: we have no server, and we cannot read anyone’s iCloud data.
  • Who can see it. Only the people the household’s creator invites, using Apple’s standard sharing sheet (a link sent by Messages or Mail). Nothing is public or searchable. Every shared history entry shows who logged it, using the display name that person chose.
  • What is not shared. Your personal things, your notification settings, your Nivli+ purchase. Members do not see each other’s personal things.
  • Apple’s role. iCloud is operated by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy and your iCloud settings. Nivli uses CloudKit only to move your data between your own devices and the people you invited; we receive nothing.
  • Silent updates. When a household changes, Apple sends the app a silent push notification so it can refresh. These carry no content, show nothing on screen, and need no permission.
  • Leaving. A member who leaves a household has the shared things removed from their iPhone. If the creator stops sharing, the household is deleted from iCloud and the things become personal things on the creator’s phone only. Switching a single thing back to personal removes it from everyone else’s phone.

What Nivli does not do

  • No account or sign-up. You are never asked for a name, email address, or password.
  • No servers. Nivli has no backend. Its only network traffic goes to Apple: the App Store for purchases and, if you use a household, iCloud.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage statistics.
  • No advertising and no tracking. Nivli does not use the advertising identifier and does not link your data with data from other apps or companies.
  • No third-party software development kits.
  • No access to Contacts, Location, or Microphone. Nivli never asks for these permissions. Your photo library is never opened by Nivli (Apple’s picker hands over only the photo you choose), and the camera is used only while you take a photo for an entry.

Notifications

If you set reminders, Nivli asks for permission to show notifications. Reminders are scheduled locally on your iPhone and contain the title of the thing you set the reminder for; nothing about your reminders leaves the device. For a shared thing, each member’s iPhone schedules its own reminder from the shared date. The only push notifications Nivli receives are Apple’s silent ones that tell the app a household changed (see above). You can turn notifications off at any time in iPhone Settings › Notifications › Nivli; the rest of the app keeps working.

Siri and Shortcuts

Nivli offers Siri phrases such as “Hey Siri, log air filter in Nivli” through Apple’s App Intents. Understanding your spoken words is done by Siri, which is part of iOS and covered by Apple’s privacy policy and your Siri settings. Once Siri has understood the request, it runs Nivli’s action on your iPhone; Nivli reads or updates its local database and gives Siri the answer. Nivli does not receive audio and does not send anything to a server. Siri and Shortcuts require no extra permission from Nivli.

Asking in your own words (Apple’s on-device model)

On iPhones with Apple Intelligence (iPhone 15 Pro and later, on iOS 26), a question that Nivli’s keyword search cannot answer is handed to Apple’s on-device model, which is part of iOS. Other iPhones keep the keyword answers, exactly as before.

  • What it receives. A short digest that Nivli builds on your iPhone from your own records: the things most related to your question, with their dates and who logged them, the next reminder, the details you saved, your notes, and the text read from your photos.
  • Where it runs. On your iPhone. Nivli uses only the model built into iOS. It makes no network request of its own, and there is no account, no server and no API key behind it.
  • What leaves the phone. Nothing. The question, the digest and the answer stay on the device, and nothing is written outside Nivli’s own storage. We receive nothing, and the App Store privacy label stays Data Not Collected.
  • The switch. Turn it off in Nivli under Settings › Siri › Answer in your own words. Nivli then answers with keyword and date matching only.

An answer names the records it used, so you can check it against your own history.

Purchases (Nivli+)

Nivli+ is sold through Apple’s in-app purchase system. Apple handles the payment, your Apple Account, receipts, renewals, and refunds under Apple’s own privacy policy. Nivli only learns from Apple whether Nivli+ is active on your device. Nivli never sees your payment card, billing address, or Apple Account details, and stores no purchase records anywhere except the status Apple provides. You can manage or cancel subscriptions in iPhone Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions.

Export

With Nivli+, you can export your history as a CSV file. The file is created on your iPhone and handed to the iOS share sheet; you choose where it goes (Files, Mail, another app). Once you send it somewhere, it is governed by that destination, not by Nivli.

Backups

Because Nivli’s data lives in the app’s storage area, it is included in your normal iPhone backup (iCloud Backup, or a backup to a computer) along with the rest of your apps. That backup is yours, under your Apple Account, and is managed by Apple, not by Nivli.

Your control and deleting your data

  • Edit or delete any thing, log, or reminder inside the app.
  • Leave a household (Settings › Household › Leave household) to remove its shared things from your iPhone, or, as the creator, stop sharing to delete the household from your iCloud.
  • Delete the app to remove all Nivli data from your iPhone. If you created a household, stop sharing first so the copy in your iCloud is deleted too; otherwise it stays in your iCloud until you do (you can also remove it under iPhone Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Manage Storage).
  • Withdraw notification permission at any time in iPhone Settings.

Since we never receive your data, there is no data we could hand over, correct, or erase on request — the only copies are on your phone and, for shared things, in your own iCloud.

Children

Nivli is rated 4+ and is a general-purpose utility. It does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Nivli ever changes how data is handled — for example, by adding a service run by us — this page and the App Store privacy label will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
