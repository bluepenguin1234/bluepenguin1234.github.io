Nivli Support

Nivli holds the apps you choose until you have won the day. Winning it means a workout, or your daily step goal, or either one — you pick the rule, and you set the number. Log a workout by hand, or connect Apple Health and let a run, a gym session or a walk do it for you. The apps open until midnight, and the day counts. Everything stays on your iPhone. Requires iOS 18 or later, iPhone only.

Nivli 3.0 is a new app. Nivli used to be a memory app for the upkeep of life. Version 3.0 replaces it with this one. If you are looking for the old product, its features are not in this version.

The basics

  • Apps that wait: the apps, categories and websites you chose during onboarding. iOS shields them; Nivli never sees their names.
  • The rule: what wins your day — a workout, a step goal, or either one. Change it any time in Settings.
  • Log a workout: the button on Home. Pick a kind and a length, tap Log it, done.
  • Apple Health: optional and read-only. A workout recorded by your Apple Watch, or by any app that writes to Health, wins the day on its own — and so does reaching your step goal.
  • Minimum: the length a workout has to reach to count. Default 20 minutes; choose 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 or 60.
  • Step goal: 5,000, 7,500, 10,000, 12,500 or 15,000 steps a day, counted by Apple Health.
  • Streak: the run of days ending today in which you either won the day or took a rest day.
  • Rest days: weekly days off, plus Take today off for a one-off.
  • Strict mode: while it is on, every change that makes the day easier waits 24 hours.
  • Reminder: an optional evening nudge if you have not won the day yet.
  • The month card: this month as a calendar on Home, and how many of its days you won.
  • Widget: the ring, your streak and whether your apps are waiting, on the Home Screen or the Lock Screen. Tap it to log a workout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I change what wins my day?

Open Nivli and tap the gear for Settings. Under How you win the day, the Win with menu switches between A workout, A step goal and Either one. Right below it are the numbers that rule uses: the step goal, and, further down, the minimum workout length. Only the numbers your rule actually measures are shown, so a step-goal day never asks you to set a workout minimum you will never be held to.

Changing the rule does not rewrite your history. Every past day is judged by the rule in force when you look at it, so switching to a stricter rule can change what an earlier week looks like — nothing is lost, it is simply read a different way.

If strict mode is on, a change that makes the day easier (a lower step goal, a shorter minimum, a rule that is easier to meet) will wait 24 hours before it takes effect. A change that makes the day harder happens immediately.

What is strict mode, and how do I cancel a change that is waiting?

Strict mode is a promise you can’t talk yourself out of later. Turn it on in Settings › Strict mode. From then on:

  • Anything that lowers the bar — a smaller step goal, a shorter minimum, an easier rule, an extra rest day, turning Apple Health off, removing apps from the list, and turning strict mode itself off — is written down and applied 24 hours later. The switch springs back and Nivli buzzes once to say “not yet”.
  • Anything that raises the bar happens at once. There is no reason to protect you from your own ambition.
  • Take today off is refused outright rather than queued. A day off that arrives tomorrow is not a day off.
  • Deleting your data, and deleting Nivli, are never delayed.

Everything that is waiting appears in a Waiting section in Settings, each line naming the change and how long it has left (“Applies in 23 h 12 m”), with a Cancel button beside it. Tapping Cancel simply drops the change — nothing has happened yet, so there is nothing to undo, and cancelling is never itself delayed. The setting it belongs to also grows a small clock line, so a switch that springs back never looks like a bug. The pill at the top of Home shows the same thing at a glance: Strict, or 2 queued · 23h.

Asking twice replaces rather than stacks: if you lower the step goal and then lower it again, one change is waiting, the second one.

My steps are not updating

Tap the steps number on Home. That is the fix, and it works every time: it asks Apple Health for today’s count there and then, and if the walk you just finished crosses your goal, your apps open on the spot.

The reason it is needed: Apple only wakes an app for a changed step count about once an hour — that is the shortest interval iOS allows for steps — so between those wake-ups the number on screen can be behind your legs. Once the reading is more than fifteen minutes old, Nivli says so in mint under the number (“Steps updated 24 min ago”), which is your cue to tap it. Opening Nivli at all also forces a fresh read.

If tapping it changes nothing, check that Steps is switched on in the Health app under Sharing › Apps › Nivli. Health permissions are all-or-nothing per type and iOS never tells an app it was refused, so this is the one thing worth checking twice.

I blocked a whole category, but some of its apps still open

Re-tick the category. Open Settings › Apps that wait (or Edit on the apps card on Home), untick the category, tick it again, and close the picker.

Why: when you tick a whole category, iOS can hand Nivli either the category on its own or the category and every app inside it. The category on its own has proved to leave some apps open. Nivli now always asks for the second kind, so ticking a category again records every app in it as well — which is also why, after re-ticking, the picker shows a tick beside each app in that category. A selection made by an older version of Nivli keeps its old shape until you edit it once.

What counts as a workout?

Any workout, from either source, that is at least as long as your minimum.

  • Logged by you. Pick one of the nine kinds (run, walk, strength, cycle, swim, yoga, HIIT, sport, other) and a length. It is an honour system; Nivli believes you.
  • From Apple Health. Any workout saved to Health today, by your Apple Watch or by another app, counted by its recorded duration.

Your minimum is in Settings, and the default is 20 minutes. A shorter session is still kept in your history, it just does not win the day. The Log sheet tells you which it will be before you save. Nivli does not look at intensity, heart rate, distance or calories, and nothing anywhere in Nivli records what time of day you won.

I worked out, but my apps are still locked

Work through these in order.

  1. Open Nivli once. If you logged the workout by hand, the shields lift straight away. If it came from Apple Health while your phone was in your pocket, opening Nivli always forces the check.
  2. Give Health a moment. A workout ended on an Apple Watch has to sync to the iPhone’s Health app before Nivli can see it. That is usually seconds, but it can take a few minutes, and it will not happen at all while the watch is out of range.
  3. Check the length. A 12-minute walk does not clear a 20-minute minimum. Home shows today’s workout and its length; lower the minimum in Settings if it is set higher than you meant.
  4. Check the day. Nivli uses your iPhone’s own calendar day, midnight to midnight. A workout finished at 11:58 pm wins that day, not the next one.
  5. Close the app and open it again. An app you already had open when the workout landed keeps running; iOS only applies and removes shields as apps are opened.

An app I blocked was already open, and it kept working

That is how iOS works and Nivli cannot change it. A shield stops an app from being opened; it cannot close one that is already running. Swipe the app away in the App Switcher and the shield appears the next time you tap its icon.

How do I change which apps are locked?

Open Nivli, go to Settings › Apps that wait, and tap to open Apple’s picker. Add or remove apps, whole categories and websites, then close the picker. You can also tap Edit on the apps card on Home. Adding more applies immediately; under strict mode, removing some waits 24 hours.

How do I stop Nivli blocking anything?

  • Keep the app, unlock everything: Settings › Apps that wait, clear the selection so nothing is chosen. With an empty selection Nivli shields nothing, on any day.
  • Just today: Settings › Take today off (not available while strict mode is on).
  • Remove it completely: delete the Nivli app. iOS lifts every shield it set and removes its data with it. You can also switch Screen Time authorization off in iPhone Settings › Screen Time.

There is no hidden hold on your phone. Nivli only shields what you picked, and only while it is installed, subscribed and set up.

How do rest days work?

In Settings › Rest days you can mark any weekdays as rest days. On a rest day nothing is locked, and your streak is neither broken nor grown. For a one-off, use Take today off on the same screen; it applies to today only and confirms first. New installs start with no rest days, so every day counts until you set some.

Apple Health is not winning the day for me

  1. Open the Health app, then Sharing › Apps › Nivli, and make sure Workouts and Steps are switched on. Health permissions are all-or-nothing per type, and iOS never tells an app it was refused, so this is the first thing to check.
  2. Check the workout is actually in Health: Browse › Activity › Workouts. If it is not there, the app or watch that recorded it did not save it, and Nivli has nothing to read.
  3. Make sure Apple Health is on inside Nivli, under Settings › Apple Health.
  4. Check the workout is long enough for your minimum and is dated today.
  5. Open Nivli once, or tap the steps number on Home. Either forces an import, whatever the background state.

Nivli only ever reads workouts and daily step totals, and never writes anything to Health. There are two switches and both matter: Settings › Apple Health inside Nivli, which is also the off switch — turning it off stops Nivli watching straight away — and Health › Sharing › Apps › Nivli, which is the only place iOS lets you grant or withdraw the read itself. If you would rather not use Health at all, logging by hand does exactly the same job — but a step goal cannot work without it, because Health is the only place a step comes from.

Where do I see how I am doing?

On Home, under today’s numbers. The month card is this month as a calendar, with a mark on every day — a filled check for a day you won, a leaf for a rest day, a plain number for a day that passed, a faint number for a day before you started, an open ring for a day still to come — and how many of its days you have won across the top. Under it, the year so far and the next streak worth reaching. There is no separate weekly report and no Sunday summary; both were removed because they did not earn their place. Nivli never records or scores what time of day you earned anything.

Can I use Nivli without a subscription?

Only during the free week. Nivli starts with 7 days free, and after that it is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year (about $2.50 a month). There is no free tier. Without a valid subscription Nivli shields nothing at all, so nothing gets stuck locked if you let it lapse. If you have bought Nivli before on this Apple Account, tap Restore Purchases on the paywall or in Settings. The free week is offered once per Apple Account, so if you have already had one you will see the ordinary price instead.

How do I cancel the free trial, or the subscription?

Both are cancelled the same way, and both are managed by Apple rather than by us: iPhone Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions › Nivli › Cancel Subscription, or apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions.

  • During the free week: cancel at least 24 hours before it ends and you are not charged at all. You keep Nivli until the seventh day. Nivli also sends you one reminder two days before the trial ends, so the date does not creep up on you.
  • After that: cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date to avoid being charged for the next period; you keep access until the period you have paid for ends.

Refunds are handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

I bought Nivli lifetime in an older version

It still works. The lifetime purchase is not sold any more, but it is honoured: tap Restore Purchases and Nivli unlocks as usual. The same goes for a yearly subscription bought before 3.0.

Can Nivli block apps on my child’s phone?

No. Nivli asks for Screen Time authorization for the iPhone it is installed on, for the person using it. It has no parental, family or child features and cannot manage another device. For that, use Apple’s own Screen Time and Family Sharing.

Can I get around it?

Easily, if you want to. You can clear your selection, take the day off, or delete the app at any moment. Strict mode makes the first two slower, not impossible. Nivli is a speed bump you agreed to, not a lock someone else holds the key to.

Does Nivli see what I do on my phone?

No. The apps you choose reach Nivli as opaque tokens that only iOS can resolve, so it cannot read their names, let alone your usage. It has no server, no analytics and no third-party code. See the privacy policy for the detail.

How do I delete my data?

Use Settings › Reset › Turn off Nivli and delete my data inside Nivli to erase your workouts, streak, selection and settings, or delete the app to remove everything at once. Neither is ever delayed, whatever strict mode says. There is no copy anywhere else, so there is nothing further to request.

I changed iPhone

Nivli’s data is stored in a file that is excluded from iCloud Backup and computer backups, because it can contain workouts read from Apple Health. Setting up a new iPhone means setting Nivli up again; your streak does not move between phones. Pick your apps again, grant Screen Time and Health on the new device, and today is day one. There is no account to sign in to, and your subscription follows your Apple Account; tap Restore Purchases if the paywall appears.
